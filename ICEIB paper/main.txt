Abstract
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[A: Attention Getter] Residential housing decisions in Taiwan require synthesizing heterogeneous transaction prices, building typologies, floor areas, spatial contexts, and evolving regulatory timelines, making intelligent big-data decision support increasingly critical. [B: But] However, publicly disclosed Actual Price Registration records do not represent complete market conditions, unit prices are easily distorted by bundled parking spaces and area definitions, and single-point predictive models obscure uncertainty, while standard retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) pipelines often produce unsupported claims when retrieved context is insufficient. [C: Cure] To address these challenges, we propose the Taiwan Housing Intelligence Agent, a domain instantiation of the DIC3 (data-driven, context-aware, collaborative decision-support) framework that combines a bounded ReAct orchestrator, versioned domain-knowledge RAG, deterministic transaction-statistics tools, and an explicit Evidence and Safety Gate. [D: Development] Developed via a spec-driven OpenSpec workflow, the proposed design enforces a five-element output contract (value, sample scope, date, source, and limitations) and triggers a deterministic Safe Stop whenever core transaction evidence is missing, expired, or schema-invalid. [E: Experiments] To evaluate our design, we conduct an initial data feasibility audit on the Taichung City Land Administration Bureau Q3 2026 (ROC Year 115 Q3) sales snapshot and establish a two-layer comparative protocol pitting bounded ReAct + RAG against a fixed deterministic workflow under identical queries and sources. [F: Findings] The completed feasibility audit (10,638 raw records across 32 fields; 9,152 filtered residential candidates) confirms a "Conditional Go" data baseline with 61.9% parking-bundled raw records, while live model training and agent benchmarking remain pending protocol execution without fabricated empirical scores.

Index Terms—Actual Price Registration, AI agent, context-aware decision support, evidence gate, explainable AI, real-estate valuation, ReAct, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), safe stop, spec-driven design.

I. INTRODUCTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[G: General] With the rapid expansion of urban open data, geospatial big data analytics, and large language model (LLM) agents, data-driven decision-support systems have achieved substantial progress across complex real-world domains. Within the DIC3 (data-driven, context-aware, collaborative decision-support) paradigm, modern analytical systems are expected to fuse heterogeneous records, temporal dynamics, and institutional constraints while preserving human oversight and accountability. In Taiwan's residential market, buyers, analysts, and researchers increasingly rely on public Actual Price Registration records to evaluate district price levels, building-type distributions, and temporal trends.

[H: However] However, transforming raw public transaction disclosures into trustworthy residential decision support presents severe technical and epistemic challenges. First, disclosed transaction prices are conditional historical observations rather than a complete, real-time census of market equilibrium; identical nominal prices frequently correspond to divergent floor areas, floor levels, parking allocations, and disclosure timings. Second, conventional machine learning valuation models typically output a single point estimate without disclosing sample boundaries, unit definitions, or epistemic uncertainty. Third, although integrating LLMs with retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) enables natural-language querying over both numerical tables and regulatory texts, unconstrained RAG agents frequently hallucinate or extrapolate unsupported price claims when retrieved context lacks sufficiency, spatial granularity, or temporal validity.

[I: In Literature] Prior research addressing these issues spans two major categories, progressing from classical statistical appraisal toward multimodal deep learning and agentic tool orchestration. In real-estate analytics, early hedonic and mass-appraisal models evolved into deep learning frameworks fusing heterogeneous transaction, spatial, and street-view imagery features [1]–[3], spatiotemporal mass appraisal [4], Taiwan macroeconomic and tax-policy analyses [5], [6], causal factor selection [7], post-hoc explainability via SHAP [8], and specialized spatiotemporal resampling and cross-validation strategies [9], [10]. In parallel, LLM grounding and agentic reasoning progressed from non-parametric RAG [11] and interleaved ReAct loops [12] to self-supervised tool learning [13], multi-model orchestration [14], structured planning [15], [16], self-reflection [17], tree search [18], and parallel function calling [19], culminating in domain-specific real-estate agents and LLM benchmarks [20], [21].

[J: Judgement] Evaluating both categories reveals complementary strengths and weaknesses. Quantitative valuation models achieve strong predictive fit on structured benchmarks but offer weak conversational adaptability, risk data leakage under naive random splits, and provide feature attributions that users often mistake for causal effects. Conversely, general-purpose LLM agents excel at flexible multi-step tool routing, yet suffer from latency overhead, unbounded reasoning loops, and unsafe generation when domain schemas, measurement units, or effective dates fail. Meanwhile, rigidly scripted query workflows guarantee deterministic execution but may lack flexibility on multi-condition queries—raising the testable research question of whether bounded ReAct + RAG outperforms a fixed workflow under identical data and governance rules.

[K: Keypoint] Guided by this assessment, the novelty of our proposed design lies in uniting spec-driven engineering (OpenSpec) with a safety-gated agentic architecture. Specifically, our core contributions are summarized as follows:
1) A spec-driven DIC3 system architecture integrating a bounded ReAct orchestrator (MAX_REACT_STEPS = 6), deterministic transaction-statistics tools, and versioned regulatory RAG;
2) An explicit Evidence and Safety Gate coupled with a Safe Stop abstention mechanism and a mandatory five-part cited output contract (value, sample scope, date, source, and limitations);
3) An audited empirical data feasibility baseline derived from the Taichung City Q3 2026 (ROC Year 115 Q3) official sales CSV snapshot (10,638 raw records; 9,152 residential candidates), establishing a "Conditional Go" governance posture;
4) A falsifiable two-layer evaluation protocol comparing bounded ReAct + RAG against a fixed workflow without fabricating unexecuted experimental outcomes.

[L: List Organization] The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section II reviews related methods in real-estate valuation and agentic AI and organizes them in a comparative summary table. Section III presents the proposed design across problem framing, system architecture, bounded execution workflow, an illustrative walkthrough example, and mathematical formulation. Section IV details the experimental design, performance metrics, completed Taichung City data feasibility audit, and verification protocol. Section V summarizes the contributions, operational boundaries, and future research directions.

II. RELATED WORK
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[M: Methods] Existing literature relevant to this study falls into two evolutionary tracks, moving from foundational statistical techniques to complex multi-agent and multimodal architectures:

1) Real-Estate Valuation, Spatial-Temporal Validation, and Explainability: Traditional appraisal relies on comparable sales and hedonic pricing, which newer studies augment with multi-source machine learning. Wang et al. [1] combined heterogeneous transaction attributes with location and visual features via a joint self-attention mechanism, while Chiu et al. [2] designed a lightweight spatiotemporal deep learning model and Wang [3] incorporated Google Street View environmental features. Chou et al. [4] examined mass appraisal under joint space-time dynamics. Within Taiwan's Actual Price Registration context [22], Hung et al. [5] analyzed urban housing price indices against macroeconomic variables, Yang [6] evaluated special tax-cooling policies, and recent local studies [23], [24] applied machine learning to Taiwan transaction records. Crucially, because housing data exhibit spatiotemporal dependence, Roberts et al. [9] and Ecker and Isakson [10] demonstrated that block and temporal validation splits are essential to avoid optimistic leakage. For interpretability, Lundberg and Lee [8] introduced SHAP for additive feature attribution, though attribution remains distinct from causal identification as noted in recent causal-agent work [7].

2) LLM Grounding, Tool-Use Agents, and Domain Benchmarks: To mitigate parametric hallucination, Lewis et al. [11] introduced RAG, and Yao et al. [12] proposed ReAct to interleave reasoning traces with executable actions. Subsequent frameworks expanded tool autonomy and planning efficiency, including Toolformer [13], HuggingGPT [14], Plan-and-Solve [15], ReWOO [16], Reflexion [17], Tree of Thoughts [18], and LLM Compiler [19]. Specifically targeting real estate, Haurum et al. [20] prototyped a LangChain-based property information agent, while Zhu and Han [21] proposed the REAL benchmark to evaluate LLM competence across housing services.

[N: New Proposed] Despite steady progress in both tracks, a critical operational gap persists: existing valuation models rarely enforce conversational provenance or abstention, whereas general-purpose LLM agents lack domain-specific schema audits for Taiwan's real-estate disclosures (such as parking-space unbundling, ROC year-month cohort semantics, and district-level de-identification). Our proposed design bridges this gap not by claiming unconstrained agent superiority, but by introducing an auditable Evidence and Safety Gate that enforces deterministic computation and Safe Stop abstention, enabling a fair head-to-head comparison between bounded ReAct + RAG and a fixed deterministic workflow.

III. PROPOSED DESIGN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. Aim and Problem Statement (PA)
The primary aim of the Taiwan Housing Intelligence Agent (THIA) is to design and evaluate a controllable, context-aware decision-support system for Taiwan residential queries that integrates structured transaction records, versioned domain documentation, and bounded tool orchestration. Formally, given a user query regarding a specified administrative district, period, building type, and comparable conditions, the system must produce an answer with traceable provenance and explicit limitations—or safely degrade without fabricating a price claim when core evidence is insufficient.

B. Background, Benefits, and Cure Rationale (PB, PC)
In operational housing environments, fragmented signals and unverified LLM outputs create tangible decision risks. Basing our architecture on the DIC3 OpenSpec workflow (Ingest -> Frame -> Model -> Verify -> Deploy -> Iterate) provides three direct benefits: (1) explicit requirement traceability prior to code execution; (2) strict separation between deterministic numerical calculation and natural-language synthesis; and (3) enforced governance over data recency and spatial granularity.

The cure rationale targets three failure modes observed in prior systems:
1) To cure single-point price overconfidence, outputs must follow a fixed five-part specification: Value/Range + Sample Scope + Date + Source + Limitations.
2) To cure unconstrained RAG hallucination, an Evidence and Safety Gate audits schema, unit, provenance, and effective dates prior to generation.
3) To cure unbounded agent execution, the ReAct loop is constrained to whitelisted tools and a strict step budget (MAX_REACT_STEPS = 6).

C. Design Paradigm and Core System Elements (PD, PE)
The proposed design shifts the paradigm from "LLM-generated valuation" to "spec-driven, evidence-gated decision orchestration," governed by five design principles: (1) task-first validation before split selection; (2) conditional price semantics; (3) granularity-aligned answer scope; (4) separation of SHAP attribution from causal claims; and (5) versioned, time-stamped evidence provenance.

Six modular elements (PE) constitute the system, summarized in Table II:
- Query Validation Layer: Parses natural-language input into a typed query (district, time window, building typology, area) and rejects out-of-scope requests (personal financial advice, guaranteed transaction prices, or individual tax/legal counsel).
- ReAct Orchestrator: Executes a bounded Thought–Action–Observation loop restricted to whitelisted tools and at most one transient retry.
- Structured Transaction Tools: Performs deterministic filtering, sample counting, descriptive statistics, and optional baseline estimation over approved CSV/database snapshots.
- RAG Domain-Knowledge Store: Retrieves MOI/land-bureau data dictionaries, parking-adjustment rules, and policy briefs tagged with source ID, version, and effective date.
- Evidence and Safety Gate: Validates schema integrity, unit consistency, provenance, temporal validity, and sample adequacy; quarantines invalid or failed optional outputs (e.g., GIS/ML modules) so they cannot contaminate core transaction comparisons.
- Cited Response / Safe Stop Module: Renders the five-element cited answer when evidence passes the gate, or executes a Safe Stop explaining the exact data gap.

F. Illustrative Walkthrough Example (PJ)
To clarify runtime behavior, consider two contrasting queries over the Taichung City 115 Q3 baseline:

1) Case 1 (Sufficient Evidence -> Cited Output): A user queries: "What is the median unit price of high-rise residential apartments (住宅大樓) in Xitun District within the 115 Q3 snapshot, and how do parking spaces affect interpretation?"
- Query Validation parses {district: Xitun, period: 115 Q3 snapshot, building_type: high-rise apartment}.
- Step 1 (Structured Tool) filters Dataset 103038 for matching candidates and deterministically computes sample count, median, and interquartile dispersion while flagging records containing bundled parking spaces.
- Step 2 (RAG Tool) retrieves the official Actual Price Registration rule explaining that unbundling parking area and price alters computed unit price per ping.
- Evidence Gate verifies schema match, Dataset 103038 provenance, publication-batch date semantics, and non-empty sample size.
- Output Renderer emits all five required components: deterministic statistics, exact sample scope, snapshot batch date ("115 Q3" with ROC year-month caveat), Dataset 103038 + MOI rule citations, and explicit limitations (historical sample != individual home appraisal).

2) Case 2 (Insufficient / Out-of-Scope Evidence -> Safe Stop): A user queries: "Predict the exact guaranteed selling price and mortgage return for Address X next quarter."
- Query Validation flags personal financial return and guaranteed pricing as out-of-scope, while de-identified public records lack exact street-number coordinates and authorized multi-period national batch data.
- Evidence Gate returns False for core evidence sufficiency and scope alignment; Algorithm 1 branches at Line 05 to Safe Stop, emitting zero price claims and detailing the exact data and authorization gaps.

G. Key Contributions and Experimental Linkage (PK, PL)
The key contributions (PK) are: (1) treating abstention (Safe Stop) as a first-class architectural invariant rather than a prompt afterthought; (2) binding every LLM narrative claim to deterministic tool outputs; and (3) isolating optional GIS/ML failures from corrupting verified transaction comparisons. Later evaluation (PL) operationalizes these invariants across controlled comparisons against a fixed workflow on identical queries.

H. Mathematical Formulation (PM)
Let q denote a user query parsed into typed constraints c = (d, tau, b, u), representing district d, time window tau, building typology b, and unit/parking specification u. Let A_safe be the finite whitelist of approved structured-data and RAG tools. Over reasoning steps t in {1, ..., T_max} with T_max = 6, the orchestrator generates an evidence trajectory E_t = {(a_i, o_i)}_{i=1}^t, where a_i in A_safe and o_i is the tool observation (retried at most once on transient failure).

Define a deterministic validity indicator v(o_i) in {0, 1} that equals 1 if and only if observation o_i passes schema validation S(o_i), provenance presence P(o_i), unit consistency U(o_i), temporal validity T(o_i), and spatial-scope alignment L(o_i):
    v(o_i) = S(o_i) * P(o_i) * U(o_i) * T(o_i) * L(o_i).               (1)

Let E_core subset E_T be the subset of validated core transaction observations satisfying v(o_i) = 1, and let n(E_core) denote the resulting eligible sample size. The Evidence and Safety Gate predicate G(E_T) in {0, 1} is defined as:
    G(E_T) = I[ |E_core| >= 1 AND n(E_core) >= n_min AND C_audit(y_draft, E_core, E_rag) = 1 ],  (2)
where I[.] is the indicator function, n_min is the minimum sample threshold, and C_audit(.) verifies that every numerical and regulatory claim in candidate response y_draft maps to a verified element in E_core union E_rag. When G(E_T) = 0, the system deterministically outputs y_stop (Safe Stop) with zero price claims.

Under this formulation, for an evaluation query set Q = Q_answerable union Q_insufficient, system reliability is measured via Safe Stop Rate on insufficient queries and Selective Citation Accuracy on non-abstained responses:
    SSR = (1 / |Q_insufficient|) * sum_{q in Q_insufficient} I[G(E_T(q)) = 0],                  (3)
    SCA = sum_{q in Q} I[G(E_T(q)) = 1 AND y(q) is fully supported] / sum_{q in Q} I[G(E_T(q)) = 1].  (4)
This guarantees by construction that any unsupported claim violates C_audit(.) and forces G(E_T) = 0 prior to user delivery.

IV. EXPERIMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. Quality and Quantity Performance Metrics (Q)
Following the two-layer evaluation framework, system quality and quantity are measured across two complementary metric suites:
1) Layer 1 (Data and Price Analytics): Sample coverage and completeness rates, descriptive statistics, Mean Absolute Error (MAE), Root Mean Square Error (RMSE), and Coefficient of Determination (R^2) evaluated strictly under temporal and spatial holdouts.
2) Layer 2 (Agent Answer Reliability): Tool legality rate, citation support rate (SCA), data timeliness compliance, unsupported price claim rate, Safe Stop rate on insufficient queries (SSR), end-to-end latency, and tool/token execution cost.

B. Related Methods for Comparison (R)
To isolate architectural value without confirmation bias, two structured comparisons are defined:
- Statistical Price Layer: District/building-type median baseline vs. linear regression and tree-based models (conditional on label and schema verification).
- Agentic Workflow Layer: Fixed deterministic query workflow vs. the proposed bounded ReAct + RAG orchestrator (T_max = 6) executing over identical queries, data snapshots, and RAG knowledge bases.

C. Experimental Setup and Completed Data Feasibility Results (S, U)
1) Experimental Setup (S): The empirical baseline uses an official single-snapshot CSV from the Taichung City Government Land Administration Bureau for ROC Year 115 Q3 (Q3 2026), published on the Taiwan Government Open Data Platform (Dataset ID: 103038).
2) Completed Data Feasibility Results (U): Prior to training predictive models or executing live LLM agents, a complete data feasibility audit was executed on Dataset 103038. As detailed in Table III, the raw file contains 10,638 records across 32 fields, from which 9,152 residential candidate records were retained using researcher-defined transaction-target and building-type inclusion rules. Notably, 6,587 of the 10,638 raw records (61.9%) bundle land, building, and parking spaces together, confirming the practical necessity of explicit parking-unit caveats. Furthermore, date-code inspection shows that within the "115 Q3" publication batch, 8,302 raw records (7,592 residential candidates) carry date codes earlier than 2026 Q3 while 2,336 raw records (1,560 candidates) fall within 2026 Q3. Because ROC year-month coding semantics require official data-dictionary verification and public locations are de-identified, the audit concludes "Conditional Go"—authorizing controlled schema/inclusion review, not immediate valuation training.

In strict adherence to academic integrity, live ML model training, ablation runs, and ReAct-vs-workflow benchmark execution have NOT yet been conducted; Table III reports only verified data-inventory measurements, and no synthetic Layer 1 or Layer 2 performance scores are claimed.

V. CONCLUSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[W: What Proposed] This paper proposed the Taiwan Housing Intelligence Agent, a spec-driven, context-aware decision-support design grounded in the DIC3 OpenSpec framework to address unreliable point valuations and ungrounded LLM outputs in Taiwan's housing market. [X: eXcel] By combining a bounded ReAct orchestrator (MAX_REACT_STEPS = 6), deterministic structured-data computation, versioned regulatory RAG, and a mathematical Evidence and Safety Gate, the architecture excels in enforcing auditability, explicit sample/parking caveats, and fail-safe Safe Stop abstention whenever core evidence is absent or mismatched. [Y: Yields] Completed empirical work established a verified feasibility baseline on the Taichung City 115 Q3 official dataset (10,638 raw records -> 9,152 residential candidates; Conditional Go), exposed critical data hazards including 61.9% parking-bundled records and batch-versus-transaction date mismatches, and delivered a falsifiable two-layer evaluation protocol. [Z: Zen / Future Work] Because live model training and agent benchmarking remain pending, future work will complete the official ROC year-month data dictionary verification, execute the Layer 1 and Layer 2 comparative experiments (H1–H3) prior to the ICEIB 2027 final deadline, evaluate user comprehension of the five-part output contract, and pursue formal national MOI batch-data authorization while maintaining strict boundaries against personal financial, legal, or guaranteed-price claims.
