\begin{abstract}
% [A: Attention Getter]
Residential housing decisions in Taiwan require synthesizing heterogeneous transaction prices, building typologies, floor areas, spatial contexts, and evolving regulatory timelines, making intelligent big-data decision support increasingly critical.
% [B: But]
However, publicly disclosed Actual Price Registration records do not represent complete market conditions, unit prices are easily distorted by bundled parking spaces and area definitions, and single-point predictive models obscure uncertainty, while standard retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) pipelines often emit unsupported claims when retrieved context is insufficient.
% [C: Cure]
To address these challenges, we propose the Taiwan Housing Intelligence Agent (THIA), a domain instantiation of the DIC3 (data-driven, context-aware, collaborative decision-support) framework that integrates a bounded ReAct orchestrator, versioned domain-knowledge RAG, deterministic transaction-statistics tools, and an explicit Evidence and Safety Gate.
% [D: Development]
Developed via a spec-driven OpenSpec workflow, the proposed design enforces a five-element output contract (value, sample scope, date, source, and limitations) and triggers a deterministic Safe Stop whenever core transaction evidence is missing, expired, or schema-invalid.
% [E: Experiments]
To evaluate our design, we conduct an empirical data feasibility audit on the Taichung City Land Administration Bureau Q3 2026 (ROC Year 115 Q3) sales snapshot and establish a two-layer comparative protocol pitting bounded ReAct + RAG against a fixed deterministic workflow under identical queries and sources.
% [F: Findings]
The completed feasibility audit (10,638 raw records across 32 fields; 9,152 filtered residential candidates) confirms a ``Conditional Go'' data baseline in which 61.9\% of raw records bundle parking spaces, while live model training and agent benchmarking remain pending protocol execution without fabricated empirical scores.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% [G: General]
With the rapid expansion of urban open data, geospatial big data analytics, and large language model (LLM) agents, data-driven decision-support systems have achieved substantial progress across complex real-world domains. Within the DIC3 (data-driven, context-aware, collaborative decision-support) paradigm, modern analytical systems are expected to fuse heterogeneous records, temporal dynamics, and institutional constraints while preserving human oversight and accountability. In Taiwan's residential market, buyers, analysts, and researchers increasingly rely on public Actual Price Registration records to evaluate district price levels, building-type distributions, and temporal trends.

% [H: However]
However, transforming raw public transaction disclosures into trustworthy residential decision support presents severe technical and epistemic challenges. First, disclosed transaction prices are conditional historical observations rather than a complete, real-time census of market equilibrium; identical nominal prices frequently correspond to divergent floor areas, floor levels, parking allocations, and disclosure timings. Second, conventional machine learning valuation models typically output a single point estimate without disclosing sample boundaries, unit definitions, or epistemic uncertainty. Third, although integrating LLMs with retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) enables natural-language querying over both numerical tables and regulatory texts, unconstrained RAG agents frequently hallucinate or extrapolate unsupported price claims when retrieved context lacks sufficiency, spatial granularity, or temporal validity.

% [I: In Literature]
Prior research addressing these issues spans two major categories, progressing from classical statistical appraisal toward multimodal deep learning and agentic tool orchestration. In real-estate analytics, early hedonic and mass-appraisal models evolved into deep learning frameworks fusing heterogeneous transaction, spatial, and street-view imagery features~\cite{wang2021,chiu2022,wang2023}, spatiotemporal mass appraisal~\cite{chou2023}, Taiwan macroeconomic and tax-policy analyses~\cite{hung2025,yang2025}, causal factor selection~\cite{chang2025}, post-hoc explainability via SHAP~\cite{lundberg2017}, and specialized spatiotemporal resampling and cross-validation strategies~\cite{roberts2017,ecker2022}. In parallel, LLM grounding and agentic reasoning progressed from non-parametric RAG~\cite{lewis2020} and interleaved ReAct loops~\cite{yao2023react} to self-supervised tool learning~\cite{schick2023}, multi-model orchestration~\cite{shen2023}, structured planning~\cite{wang2023plan,xu2023}, self-reflection~\cite{shinn2023}, tree search~\cite{yao2023tot}, and parallel function calling~\cite{kim2024}, culminating in domain-specific real-estate agents and LLM benchmarks~\cite{haurum2024,zhu2025}.

% [J: Judgement]
Evaluating both categories reveals complementary strengths and weaknesses. Quantitative valuation models achieve strong predictive fit on structured benchmarks but offer weak conversational adaptability, risk data leakage under naive random splits, and provide feature attributions that users often mistake for causal effects. Conversely, general-purpose LLM agents excel at flexible multi-step tool routing, yet suffer from latency overhead, unbounded reasoning loops, and unsafe generation when domain schemas, measurement units, or effective dates fail. Meanwhile, rigidly scripted query workflows guarantee deterministic execution but may lack flexibility on multi-condition queries---raising the testable research question of whether bounded ReAct + RAG outperforms a fixed workflow under identical data and governance rules.

% [K: Keypoint]
Guided by this assessment, the novelty of our proposed design lies in uniting spec-driven engineering (OpenSpec) with a safety-gated agentic architecture. Specifically, our core contributions are fourfold:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item A spec-driven DIC3 system architecture integrating a bounded ReAct orchestrator (\texttt{MAX\_REACT\_STEPS = 6}), deterministic transaction-statistics tools, and versioned regulatory RAG;
    \item An explicit Evidence and Safety Gate coupled with a Safe Stop abstention mechanism and a mandatory five-part cited output contract (value, sample scope, date, source, and limitations);
    \item An audited empirical data feasibility baseline derived from the Taichung City Q3 2026 (ROC Year 115 Q3) official sales CSV snapshot (10,638 raw records; 9,152 residential candidates), establishing a ``Conditional Go'' governance posture;
    \item A falsifiable two-layer evaluation protocol comparing bounded ReAct + RAG against a fixed workflow without fabricating unexecuted experimental outcomes.
\end{enumerate}

% [L: List Organization]
The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section~II reviews related methods in real-estate valuation and agentic AI and organizes them in a comparative summary table. Section~III presents the proposed design across problem framing, system architecture, bounded execution workflow, an illustrative walkthrough example, and mathematical formulation. Section~IV details the experimental design, performance metrics, completed Taichung City data feasibility audit, and verification protocol. Section~V summarizes the contributions, operational boundaries, and future research directions.

\section{Related Work}
% [M: Methods]
Existing literature relevant to this study falls into two evolutionary tracks, moving from foundational statistical techniques to complex multi-agent and multimodal architectures.

\subsection{Real-Estate Valuation, Spatial-Temporal Validation, and Explainability}
Traditional appraisal relies on comparable sales and hedonic pricing, which newer studies augment with multi-source machine learning. Wang et al.~\cite{wang2021} combined heterogeneous transaction attributes with location and visual features via a joint self-attention mechanism, while Chiu et al.~\cite{chiu2022} designed a lightweight spatiotemporal deep learning model and Wang~\cite{wang2023} incorporated Google Street View environmental features. Chou et al.~\cite{chou2023} examined mass appraisal under joint space-time dynamics. Within Taiwan's Actual Price Registration context~\cite{moi2026}, Hung et al.~\cite{hung2025} analyzed urban housing price indices against macroeconomic variables, Yang~\cite{yang2025} evaluated special tax-cooling policies, and recent local studies~\cite{lin2024,chang2024} applied machine learning to Taiwan transaction records. Crucially, because housing data exhibit spatiotemporal dependence, Roberts et al.~\cite{roberts2017} and Ecker and Isakson~\cite{ecker2022} demonstrated that block and temporal validation splits are essential to avoid optimistic leakage. For interpretability, Lundberg and Lee~\cite{lundberg2017} introduced SHAP for additive feature attribution, though attribution remains distinct from causal identification as noted in recent causal-agent work~\cite{chang2025}.

\subsection{LLM Grounding, Tool-Use Agents, and Domain Benchmarks}
To mitigate parametric hallucination, Lewis et al.~\cite{lewis2020} introduced RAG, and Yao et al.~\cite{yao2023react} proposed ReAct to interleave reasoning traces with executable actions. Subsequent frameworks expanded tool autonomy and planning efficiency, including Toolformer~\cite{schick2023}, HuggingGPT~\cite{shen2023}, Plan-and-Solve~\cite{wang2023plan}, ReWOO~\cite{xu2023}, Reflexion~\cite{shinn2023}, Tree of Thoughts~\cite{yao2023tot}, and LLM Compiler~\cite{kim2024}. Specifically targeting real estate, Haurum et al.~\cite{haurum2024} prototyped a LangChain-based property information agent, while Zhu and Han~\cite{zhu2025} proposed the REAL benchmark to evaluate LLM competence across housing services.

% [N: New Proposed]
\subsection{Research Gap and Comparative Summary}
Despite steady progress in both tracks, a critical operational gap persists: existing valuation models rarely enforce conversational provenance or abstention, whereas general-purpose LLM agents lack domain-specific schema audits for Taiwan's real-estate disclosures (such as parking-space unbundling, ROC year-month cohort semantics, and district-level de-identification). Our proposed design bridges this gap not by claiming unconstrained agent superiority, but by introducing an auditable Evidence and Safety Gate that enforces deterministic computation and Safe Stop abstention, enabling a fair head-to-head comparison between bounded ReAct + RAG and a fixed deterministic workflow.

\section{Proposed Design}
% [PA: Aim]
\subsection{Aim and Problem Statement}
The primary aim of the Taiwan Housing Intelligence Agent (THIA) is to design and evaluate a controllable, context-aware decision-support system for Taiwan residential queries that integrates structured transaction records, versioned domain documentation, and bounded tool orchestration. Formally, given a user query regarding a specified administrative district, time period, building typology, and comparable conditions, the system must produce an answer with traceable provenance and explicit limitations---or safely degrade without fabricating a price claim when core evidence is insufficient.

% [PB: Based on / Benefit, PC: Cure / Cause]
\subsection{Background, Benefits, and Cure Rationale}
In operational housing environments, fragmented signals and unverified LLM outputs create tangible decision risks. Grounding our architecture in the DIC3 OpenSpec workflow ($\text{Ingest} \rightarrow \text{Frame} \rightarrow \text{Model} \rightarrow \text{Verify} \rightarrow \text{Deploy} \rightarrow \text{Iterate}$) yields three direct benefits: (1) explicit requirement traceability prior to code execution; (2) strict separation between deterministic numerical computation and natural-language synthesis; and (3) enforced governance over data recency and spatial granularity.

The cure rationale directly addresses three failure modes observed in prior systems:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item To cure single-point price overconfidence, outputs must follow a fixed five-part specification: Value/Range, Sample Scope, Date, Source, and Limitations.
    \item To cure unconstrained RAG hallucination, an Evidence and Safety Gate audits schema, unit, provenance, and effective dates prior to generation.
    \item To cure unbounded agent execution, the ReAct loop is constrained to whitelisted tools and a strict step budget (\texttt{MAX\_REACT\_STEPS = 6}).
\end{enumerate}

% [PD: Design Paradigm, PE: Paper Elements]
\subsection{Design Paradigm and Core System Elements}
The proposed design shifts the paradigm from ``LLM-generated valuation'' to ``spec-driven, evidence-gated decision orchestration,'' governed by five design principles: (1) task-first validation before split selection; (2) conditional price semantics; (3) granularity-aligned answer scope; (4) separation of SHAP attribution from causal claims; and (5) versioned, time-stamped evidence provenance.

Six modular elements constitute the system, summarized in Table~\ref{tab:elements}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{Query Validation Layer}: Parses natural-language input into a typed query (district, time window, building typology, area) and rejects out-of-scope requests (personal financial advice, guaranteed transaction prices, or individual tax/legal counsel).
    \item \emph{ReAct Orchestrator}: Executes a bounded Thought--Action--Observation loop restricted to whitelisted tools and at most one transient retry.
    \item \emph{Structured Transaction Tools}: Performs deterministic filtering, sample counting, descriptive statistics, and optional baseline estimation over approved CSV/database snapshots.
    \item \emph{RAG Domain-Knowledge Store}: Retrieves MOI/land-bureau data dictionaries, parking-adjustment rules, and policy briefs tagged with source ID, version, and effective date.
    \item \emph{Evidence and Safety Gate}: Validates schema integrity, unit consistency, provenance, temporal validity, and sample adequacy; quarantines invalid or failed optional outputs (e.g., GIS/ML modules) so they cannot contaminate core transaction comparisons.
    \item \emph{Cited Response / Safe Stop Module}: Renders the five-element cited answer when evidence passes the gate, or executes a Safe Stop explaining the exact data gap.
\end{itemize}

% [PJ: Jump to an example]
\subsection{Illustrative Walkthrough Example}
To clarify runtime behavior, consider two contrasting queries over the Taichung City 115 Q3 baseline:

\subsubsection{Case 1 (Sufficient Evidence $\rightarrow$ Cited Output)}
A user asks: ``What is the median unit price of high-rise residential apartments in Xitun District within the 115 Q3 snapshot, and how do parking spaces affect interpretation?'' Query Validation extracts \{\textit{district}: Xitun, \textit{period}: 115 Q3 snapshot, \textit{building\_type}: high-rise apartment\}. Step~1 invokes the Structured Transaction Tool over Dataset~103038 to compute deterministic sample counts, medians, and dispersion while flagging bundled parking transactions. Step~2 invokes the RAG Store to retrieve the Actual Price Registration rule explaining how unbundling parking area and price alters computed unit price per ping. The Evidence Gate verifies schema match, Dataset~103038 provenance, publication-batch date semantics, and sufficient sample size, producing the mandatory five-part output (statistics, sample scope, batch date caveat, official citations, and limitations).

\subsubsection{Case 2 (Insufficient / Out-of-Scope Evidence $\rightarrow$ Safe Stop)}
A user asks: ``Predict the exact guaranteed selling price and mortgage return for Street Address X next quarter.'' Query Validation flags personal financial return and guaranteed pricing as out-of-scope, while de-identified public records lack exact house-number coordinates and authorized multi-period national batch data. Because valid core evidence fails the Evidence Gate, Line~05 triggers Safe Stop, emitting zero price claims and reporting the exact scope and data-authorization gaps.

% [PK: Key points contribution, PL: Later how to do simulation]
\subsection{Key Contributions and Experimental Linkage}
The key architectural contributions are threefold: (1) elevating abstention (Safe Stop) to a hard structural invariant rather than a soft prompt instruction; (2) binding every LLM narrative claim to deterministic tool outputs; and (3) isolating optional GIS/ML failures from corrupting verified transaction comparisons. These invariants directly feed our comparative experimental protocol in Section~IV, where gated ReAct + RAG is benchmarked against both ungated baselines and a fixed workflow.

% [PM: Math proof / Formalization]
\subsection{Mathematical Formulation}
Let $q$ denote a user query parsed into typed constraints $c = (d, \tau, b, u)$, representing district $d$, time window $\tau$, building typology $b$, and unit/parking specification $u$. Let $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{safe}}$ be the finite whitelist of approved structured-data and RAG tools. Over reasoning steps $t \in \{1, \dots, T_{\max}\}$ with $T_{\max} = 6$, the orchestrator generates an evidence trajectory $E_t = \{(a_i, o_i)\}_{i=1}^{t}$, where $a_i \in \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{safe}}$ and $o_i$ is the corresponding tool observation (retried at most once on transient error).

Define a deterministic observation validity indicator $v(o_i) \in \{0, 1\}$ that equals $1$ if and only if $o_i$ satisfies schema validation $S(o_i)$, provenance presence $P(o_i)$, unit consistency $U(o_i)$, temporal validity $T(o_i)$, and spatial-scope alignment $L(o_i)$:
\begin{equation}
v(o_i) = S(o_i) \cdot P(o_i) \cdot U(o_i) \cdot T(o_i) \cdot L(o_i).
\end{equation}
Let $E_{\mathrm{core}} \subseteq E_T$ denote the subset of validated core transaction observations with $v(o_i)=1$, and let $n(E_{\mathrm{core}})$ be the eligible sample size. The Evidence and Safety Gate predicate $G(E_T) \in \{0, 1\}$ is defined as:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
G(E_T) = \mathbb{I}\!\Big[\, |E_{\mathrm{core}}| \ge 1 &\;\wedge\; n(E_{\mathrm{core}}) \ge n_{\min} \\
&\;\wedge\; C_{\mathrm{audit}}(y_{\mathrm{draft}}, E_{\mathrm{core}}, E_{\mathrm{rag}}) = 1 \,\Big],
\end{split}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbb{I}[\cdot]$ is the indicator function, $n_{\min}$ is the minimum sample threshold, and $C_{\mathrm{audit}}(\cdot) \in \{0,1\}$ verifies that every numerical and regulatory claim in candidate response $y_{\mathrm{draft}}$ maps to a verified item in $E_{\mathrm{core}} \cup E_{\mathrm{rag}}$. When $G(E_T)=0$, the system outputs $y_{\mathrm{stop}}$ (Safe Stop) with zero price claims.

For an evaluation suite $\mathcal{Q} = \mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{ans}} \cup \mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{insuf}}$, system reliability is evaluated using the Safe Stop Rate ($\mathrm{SSR}$) on insufficient queries and Selective Citation Accuracy ($\mathrm{SCA}$) on non-abstained responses:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{SSR} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{insuf}}|} \sum_{q \in \mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{insuf}}} \mathbb{I}\!\left[G(E_T(q)) = 0\right],
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{SCA} = \frac{\sum_{q \in \mathcal{Q}} \mathbb{I}\!\left[G(E_T(q)) = 1 \;\wedge\; \mathrm{Sup}(y(q))\right]}{\sum_{q \in \mathcal{Q}} \mathbb{I}\!\left[G(E_T(q)) = 1\right]},
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{Sup}(y(q))$ denotes that every claim in $y(q)$ is fully supported by verified evidence.
By construction, any unverified numerical or regulatory claim yields $C_{\mathrm{audit}}(\cdot)=0$, forcing $G(E_T)=0$ prior to user delivery.

\section{Experiment}
% [Q: Quality and Quantity]
\subsection{Quality and Quantity Performance Metrics}
Under our two-layer evaluation framework, system quality and quantity are measured across two complementary metric suites:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{Layer 1 (Data and Price Analytics)}: Sample coverage and completeness rates, descriptive statistics, Mean Absolute Error (MAE), Root Mean Square Error (RMSE), and Coefficient of Determination ($R^2$) evaluated strictly under temporal and spatial holdouts.
    \item \emph{Layer 2 (Agent Answer Reliability)}: Tool legality rate, citation support rate ($\mathrm{SCA}$), data timeliness compliance, unsupported price claim rate, Safe Stop rate on insufficient queries ($\mathrm{SSR}$), end-to-end latency, and tool/token execution cost.
\end{itemize}

% [R: Related Methods to Compare]
\subsection{Related Methods for Comparison}
To isolate architectural value without confirmation bias, two structured comparisons are established: (1) on Layer~1, a district/building-type median baseline versus linear regression and tree-based models (conditional on schema and label verification); and (2) on Layer~2, a fixed deterministic query workflow versus the proposed bounded ReAct + RAG orchestrator ($T_{\max} = 6$) over identical queries, snapshots, and knowledge bases.

% [S: Simulation Setup, U: Useful Results (Completed Data Audit + Integrity Status)]
\subsection{Experimental Setup and Completed Data Feasibility Results}
The empirical baseline uses an official single-snapshot CSV from the Taichung City Government Land Administration Bureau for ROC Year 115 Q3 (Q3 2026), published on the Taiwan Government Open Data Platform (Dataset ID: 103038). Prior to model training or live agent benchmarking, we executed a complete data feasibility audit on Dataset~103038, summarized in Table~\ref{tab:audit}.

The raw snapshot contains 10,638 records across 32 fields, from which 9,152 residential candidate records were retained via researcher-defined transaction-target and building-type rules. Notably, 6,587 of the 10,638 raw records (61.9\%) bundle land, building, and parking spaces together, empirically confirming the necessity of explicit parking-unit caveats. Furthermore, date-code inspection shows that within the ``115 Q3'' publication batch, 8,302 raw records (7,592 residential candidates) carry date codes earlier than 2026 Q3 while 2,336 raw records (1,560 candidates) fall within 2026 Q3. Because ROC year-month coding semantics require official data-dictionary verification and public locations are de-identified, the audit concludes ``Conditional Go''---authorizing controlled schema and inclusion review rather than immediate valuation training.

In strict adherence to research integrity, live ML model training, ablation runs, and ReAct-vs-workflow benchmark execution have \emph{not} yet been conducted; Table~\ref{tab:audit} reports only verified data-inventory measurements, and no synthetic Layer~1 or Layer~2 performance scores are claimed.

\section{Conclusion}
% [W: What Proposed]
This paper proposed the Taiwan Housing Intelligence Agent (THIA), a spec-driven, context-aware decision-support design grounded in the DIC3 OpenSpec framework to address unreliable point valuations and ungrounded LLM outputs in Taiwan's housing market.
% [X: eXcel]
By combining a bounded ReAct orchestrator (\texttt{MAX\_REACT\_STEPS = 6}), deterministic structured-data computation, versioned regulatory RAG, and a mathematical Evidence and Safety Gate, the architecture excels in enforcing auditability, explicit sample and parking caveats, and fail-safe Safe Stop abstention whenever core evidence is absent or mismatched.
% [Y: Yields]
Completed empirical work established a verified feasibility baseline on the Taichung City 115 Q3 official dataset (10,638 raw records $\rightarrow$ 9,152 residential candidates; ``Conditional Go''), exposed critical data hazards including 61.9\% parking-bundled records and batch-versus-transaction date mismatches, and delivered a falsifiable two-layer evaluation protocol.
% [Z: Zen / Future Work]
Because live model training and agent benchmarking remain pending, future work will complete the official ROC year-month data dictionary verification, execute the Layer~1 and Layer~2 comparative experiments (H1--H3) prior to the ICEIB 2027 final deadline, evaluate user comprehension of the five-part output contract, and pursue formal national MOI batch-data authorization while maintaining strict boundaries against personal financial, legal, or guaranteed-price claims.
